      Where invites appear

      Never as a pop-up. A quiet line at the moments when you'd naturally think of someone.

      The reward rule

      Two ways to invite. A plain "join us" pays 2 TimeCoins when they sign in. A chair at a table pays 5 when you sit together.

      +2Your friend signs inNow. Counted once their email is confirmed. Your friend gets 2 as well.
      +5You invite them to a table and sit togetherReplaces the 2, not on top of it. Your friend gets 5 too.
      50Most you can receive in a weekTen friends a week, 5 each at most.

      Why this shape

      • Easy to understand. One link, one number. A sign-in is clear to everyone, a "finished table" would need explaining.
      • Sitting together is worth more. The table invite pays 5, because a friend who talks with you is the point.
      • It stays small next to tips. One table gives each person 8 TimeCoins to award, so inviting never outweighs being good company.
      • Nobody is ranked. No leaderboard, no invite count on profiles. The circle of ten chairs is only on your own page.

      Risks and how the rule answers them

      Fake accounts to collect coinsThis is the main risk of paying for sign-ins. One account per Apple, Google or confirmed email, coins only after the email is confirmed, and ten friends a week caps the gain. If it is abused, coins can wait until the friend's first table instead.
      Reputation bought by recruitingCoins from friends are marked "Welcome" in your history and never count toward the Trusted badge. Alternative: give them as extra coins to award at your next table instead.
      Pressure and spam on friendsNo contact import, no automatic reminders. You can remind once, by hand. "Not for me" is silent, the inviter isn't told.
      PrivacyYou see only that they signed in. Never what your friend said, how they rated, or which tables they joined.
